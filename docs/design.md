# Design direction (Iris)

The app replaces BRAVIA Connect day to day, with a Google TV–grade remote at its heart. Setup and room calibration stay in BRAVIA Connect.

## Principle: calm on top, deep underneath, and always say what unlocks more

Erwann, 2026-10-24: "Il faut vraiment que ce soit une app à la fois amazing UX et une app de geek dans les options. Assister aussi l'utilisateur pour lui dire ce qu'il doit faire s'il veut plus d'options, genre PSK etc."

- **The surface is calm.** The remote, the Son sheet and the scenes are for every evening: no protocol words, no ports, no readouts nobody asked for. A missing capability is never a dead control or a silent absence; it is one plain line saying why, with a way forward.
- **Depth lives one level down.** Everything an enthusiast wants (diagnostics, technical details, the readouts and hidden options the reverse map brings, `docs/reverse/bravia-connect.md`) sits in Settings › Advanced, behind developer mode. It may grow as large as it likes; it never leaks onto the everyday screens.
- **tutti always tells you what unlocks more, and how.** What tutti can do depends on setup done on the TV and in BRAVIA Connect. Each such step has one home, "What tutti can do" (below), that says whether it is done, what it unlocks, the exact menu path, and a button into tutti's own field for it. Wherever a missing step blocks something, the message that says so offers "Show me how", opening that step.

## Information architecture

Three places, opening on the remote.

1. **Remote (home)**
   - Top: one system chip, "Salon · BRAVIA 8 + Bar 9", single status dot; it splits into two lines only when one device drops ("Bar 9 injoignable"). One Power button switches both.
   - Below: a horizontal strip of scene pills.
   - Under the pills, in the room the pad leaves: the now-showing card (Iris's option A, chosen by Erwann, 2026-10-24), centred in that room so the band reads as meant, never as a void. Full width less 16 dp margins: the app's icon (or the state's glyph), "On the TV", the name large and a small detail line. It says an app, a channel and programme, or an HDMI input (`nowShowing`), else "TV on standby" (with "The bar stays on" when the bar plays), the home screen, or "TV out of reach"; never empty, and every state is told by its icon and words, never colour alone. Full is 104 dp tall and grows to 140 dp (larger icon and name) when the room allows, with at least 12 dp above and below; below that room it shrinks to one 56 dp line, and below that it hides (`placePad`, `cardFor`). While it shows, the system chip drops its "On the TV" line; hidden, the chip gets it back. A tap does nothing for now. State changes cross-fade by opacity only, which is also the reduced-motion form.
   - The pad mode switch (K1, Erwann, 2026-10-24): one quiet glyph on the pad's surface, in its top corner facing the rail (top-left for the right hand, top-right for the left), that leaves the pad's shape alone. It shows the mode a tap switches to, as that mode's outline icon (the touchpad's in D-pad mode, the D-pad's in touchpad mode), 24 dp, on-surface-variant, 16 dp in from the top and side edges, with no chip, border or label; the pad already says the current mode. A 48 dp hit area round it; pressed, the glyph tints and tightens. A still tap there switches with a select haptic and never reaches the TV as OK; a touch that starts there and moves is an ordinary swipe, and a swipe crossing it tracks as normal. In D-pad mode the corner is the switch's alone: no direction answers there (`dpadZone`), and the drawn disc shrinks a little on a near-square pad so it never reaches the glyph, on either side (`dpadFaceRadius`, tested). The surface stays put; only what is on it changes: the D-pad's disc fades in on a spring (scaling from 0.92), or by a fade alone when motion is reduced. One button to TalkBack: the state is the current mode, the action "Switch to touchpad" / "Switch to D-pad", with an action for each mode.
   - Low, within the thumb's reach (Erwann, 2026-10-24: the thumb reaches about half the screen): the pad, the largest element, under the holding hand's thumb, with the volume rail on its other side at exactly its height, both standing 24 dp above the button row. Right hand (the default, Erwann, 2026-10-24): pad on the right, rail on the left. Left hand: pad on the left, rail on the right. TalkBack reads the pad, then the rail, on either side. A 180 dp band along the pad's outer side, centred, keeps Android's back gesture off it (`padEdgeExclusion`, under the 200 dp per-edge budget), so a swipe from the pad's edge steps. Free room goes above the pad, as the now-showing card's room, never below the pad, and the pad never grows taller than its width (Compact 0.85, Large 1.2), so a tall screen doesn't stretch it upward (`placePad`). Touchpad or D-pad. Tap = OK. Long-press Select is reserved for the TV (sends a long press).
   - Under the pad: Back, Home, Play/Pause, Mic, Keyboard, spread evenly, Back nearest the holding thumb: for the right hand the row reads mirrored, Keyboard to Back from left to right (Erwann, 2026-10-24: Back and Home are the most used); the left hand reads it as listed. The stored order is the same for both hands (`RemoteLayout.rowOnScreen`). Mute is the volume rail's foot: the speaker there is a 48 dp key on a faint disc, the icon at full strength; a tap toggles mute, muted shows the slashed speaker over the hatched fill, and with the bar unreachable the rail is a hollow track and the speaker can't be tapped. The rail's drag starts only past touch slop, so a tap never moves the level, and a drag that starts on the speaker cancels the tap. The mic is hold to talk to the TV's assistant: it grows and fills with the primary colour while listening, a pill above it reads "À l'écoute… relâchez pour envoyer"; sliding a finger off turns it to the error container ("Relâchez pour annuler"), with a tick at the edge. A tap shorter than 300 ms sends nothing and explains the gesture. (Maya's placement, pending Iris's review.)
   - Beside the pad, on the side away from the thumb: vertical volume rail (soundbar volume, absolute). It always takes the pad's height, top and foot level with it, the same size and place in both modes (Erwann, 2026-10-24: "la taille du volume devrait être toujours la même, mais aussi D-pad et touchpad"; this reverses the same day's rail that stood in to the D-pad's disc). Drag anywhere on it to move the level. Three keys on it, one family (Erwann, 2026-10-24: "un bouton + et -, possiblement en haut et en bas"): + at the top, − just above the speaker, the mute speaker at the foot where it always was, each a 48 dp target on a solid 36 dp disc in the surface colour with a hairline ring and its icon at full strength, so it reads the same over the fill and the empty track ("pas ouf la transparence de l'icône"). + and − step the bar by one on press (a tick, the bump and the rail's stretch at a wall) and repeat on hold at the D-pad's rhythm; a drag that starts on a key still moves the rail. Muted: the speaker's slash, its disc inverted, the fill greyed and hatched. The level's number sits under + and always shows while the bar is connected ("je veux que le niveau de volume reste affiché"): the controller keeps the last level until the bar echoes a new one, live from the bar's notifies, + and − and drags; drawn twice and cut at the fill's edge (ink above, the fill's ink below), so a number the edge crosses stays legible; an ellipsis while the level is not reported yet (a press of + or − then asks the bar for it); no number while the bar is unreachable, never a stale level and never a dash that reads as the − key. Unreachable: a hollow track, no fill, the keys without discs and disabled, + and − dimmed, the speaker crossed. TalkBack: the rail is a slider (its value, set by swipe or volume keys), the keys are "Volume up", "Volume down" and "Mute".
   - Bottom: a dock of 4 shortcut slots.
   - Peek sheet "Son" at the bottom; pulled up it shows sound controls.
2. **Scenes**: gallery and editor.
3. **Réglages**: devices and pairing (a TV but no bar: "Add the soundbar", the onboarding's Bar step alone, BRAVIA Connect's External control, the address and the three checks, then back to the remote, never through the TV's steps again (Erwann, 2026-10-24: "c'est dur de revenir sur le onboarding"); no TV: "Add a device", the whole flow; with a TV, "Run setup again" walks it all from the search), TV settings (picture, sound output, inputs, power saving), soundbar system settings, app preferences, haptics.

### What tutti can do (the setup checklist)

Settings' first row, "What tutti can do", reads the progress in words ("2 of 4 set up · next: IP control and PSK", or "All set up") and opens one screen (`ui/settings/UnlockScreen.kt`; the rules, pure and tested, in `Unlocks.kt` / `UnlocksTest`). A short intro ("The remote works as soon as the TV is paired…"), then one group per device:

- **TV**: *TV remote* (pairing, 6466/6467: the base), *IP control and PSK* (TV settings, what's playing and on which input, the TV's own apps and icons, waking a TV that is fully off, since IP control is where tutti learns its hardware address), *Remote start* (the TV stays reachable in standby, read from `getWolMode`).
- **Soundbar**: *Soundbar* (External control in BRAVIA Connect, TCP 33336: the rail, mute, the Son sheet, the bar in scenes, one Power).
- **For the curious**: *Developer mode*. Optional, outside the count.
- **Sony account**: *Sony sign-in*, "Coming later", nothing to do. Optional, outside the count; it becomes a real step when phase 2 of the reverse map lands.

Each step is one row: a status disc, the title, the status in a word, and what it unlocks in one small line under it. The status never rests on colour (Erwann is colourblind): each has its own glyph and word, done (filled disc, tick, "Done"), not yet (empty ring, "Not yet"), set up but failing (warning triangle, "Needs a look", plus "Now: PSK refused or missing" in the link's own words), checking (circling arrows), waiting on an earlier step (padlock, "Pair the TV first"), unknown (question mark, "Can't tell from here": Remote start before IP control answers), coming (timer). Only "Done" also fills its disc in the primary container, as emphasis on top of the tick.

One step is open at a time, and the screen opens on the one asked for (a nudge's) or else the next to do, failing steps before undone ones. Open, it shows "How" with the exact path on the TV or in BRAVIA Connect, numbered, and one button into tutti's own field: "Enter the PSK" (the PSK dialog in place), "Pair the TV", "Add the soundbar" (the onboarding's Bar step alone), "Turn on developer mode". Done steps keep a quiet way back ("Change the PSK", "Turn it off"); IP control done adds whether waking from off is ready. Menu paths are Sony's names in each language: English "Settings → Network & Internet → Local network setup → IP control", "Normal and Pre-Shared Key", "Remote start"; French "Paramètres → Réseau et Internet → Configuration du réseau local → Contrôle IP", « Normale et clé pré-partagée », « Démarrage à distance », and BRAVIA Connect's "Contrôle externe (External control)".

Nudges, where a missing step is the cause: a failed wake carries "Show me how" (no hardware address learnt → IP control; asleep or not found → Remote start; the bar silent → Soundbar), which takes the message's one action ahead of "Copy details"; the TV settings explainer adds "See everything tutti can do"; with no bar, the Son sheet and Settings already offer "Add the soundbar".

### Advanced

Settings' last technical group is "Advanced": the developer-mode switch, and with it on, Diagnostics and Technical details (`TechnicalScreen`: per device, the address, each link, whether a PSK is stored and whether the hardware address is learnt, never their values, Remote start, the model and generation, the features the bar refused; tutti's version). The reverse map's phase-2 readouts and hidden options join here as rows or screens of their own. Nothing in Advanced is needed day to day, and nothing from it appears elsewhere.

### Leaving setup

Opened from an app already set up (Settings' "Run setup again", "Add a device", "Add the soundbar", the Son sheet's or a checklist button), setup has a Back at the top left (48 dp, "Leave setup" to TalkBack), and the system back does the same: any pairing in progress is dropped and you land where you came from, nothing changed. Inside the flow, back from the code still steps back to the search first. The very first launch has no such exit: there is nowhere to go back to (Erwann, 2026-10-24: "il faudrait pas un bouton annuler ou retour ici ?").

One tap from the remote: power, volume, mute, input, keyboard, any scene, any shortcut, Night, Voice.
Two levels deep at most.

### "Son" sheet
Sound field, Voice, Night, Auto volume, 360 Spatial (and Voice Zoom 3 when offered) as large toggle tiles: only real on/off settings are tiles. DRC and IMAX are three-way (Auto, On, Off), so each is a labelled row of three segments under the tiles, every option in view and the current one ticked and filled, read by TalkBack as the setting then each option as a radio button, as the bar settings screen lists the same choices (Erwann, 2026-10-24: "DRC est bizarre car c'est un toggle mais en fait il y a trois options"); Bass and Rear level as sliders; Voice Zoom 3 as a level slider only if the bar answers the capability probe. The rule is general (`soundSheetLayout`, tested): only a `Toggle` is a tile, every `Choice` gets all its options, a `Level` is a slider. Every documented value is listed, and a value the bar refuses (`ERR`/`NAK`) or never answers is rolled back to the confirmed one and remembered for the session (`TheatreState.refusedValues`): its segment, or its line in the bar settings menu, wears the warning glyph and the word "refused", the row says "The bar refused Auto. Tap it to try again.", and it stays tappable as a retry (Erwann, 2026-10-24: "tout lister et afficher une erreur quand l'option ne marche pas"). A greyed setting always shows its reason ("Voix désactivée : virtualiseur actif"), never hidden. "Recalibrer dans BRAVIA Connect" deep link. With no bar added, "Add the soundbar" sits under the title and opens the onboarding's Bar step alone.

### Sheets
Every sheet (Son, All keys, Apps, a pin's editor, the action picker) can rise to just below the status bar and no further: its handle and title always show, and what doesn't fit scrolls inside the sheet. Dragging down from the top of that scroll moves the sheet. Modal sheets go through `TuttiSheet` (`ui/common/Sheets.kt`).

### Shortcuts
- Dock: four pins a page, up to three pages (swipe; dots only from page 2). Each pin has a name, an icon or two letters, a tap action (one action, or ordered steps with waits, tried from the editor with "Try") and its own long-press action, offered first in a menu above "Edit this pin".
- Long-press on any dock slot or secondary button (Home, Back, Play) assigns a second action.
- Pad gestures, two only: two-finger tap = play/pause; two-finger vertical drag = bar volume.

### Edit mode
- Settings › Shortcuts › "Remote layout", or "Edit the remote layout" at the foot of a pin's editor, turns the remote into its own edit mode: the same screen, what can change in a dashed frame, what can't under a lock. Changes save as they are made; "Done" (or Back) leaves.
- Adjustable: the round-button row (up to six, from Back, Home, Play/Pause, Voice, Keyboard, Info, Subtitles, Input, Guide, TV settings; drag sideways or TalkBack's move left / right), the scene strip (shown or hidden), the pad in three steps (Compact is shorter, Normal is as tall as it is wide, Large takes the margins back and stands taller; the rail follows the pad's height in each), the hand (Left / Right, one switch; it mirrors the pad, the rail and the round-button row, never Power, the scenes, the dock or the Son sheet; a move in the editor lands where it was dropped on either hand), and the pins' order within a page (drag, or move left / right; tap opens the pin's editor).
- Anchors never move or hide: Power, the volume rail, the pad's place and the Son sheet. The pad's side is chosen once, by the hand, never dragged; the rail takes the other side. "Reset the layout" asks first and puts the row, the scenes, the pad and the hand (right) back; pins stay.
- Stored with every field defaulted to today's screen, so a phone that never opens edit mode sees no change.

## The TV's link: calm, then honest

Iris, 2026-10-24, from Erwann's "si les touches ne marchent pas à ce moment-là, peut-être que c'est mieux". The rule is `TvLinkRule` (`controller/TvLinkRule.kt`, tested); the remote reads its result, `Links.tvShown`, and Technical details keep the raw link.

- Before: every drop of the remote v2 link showed at once (chip "BRAVIA 8 unreachable" in red, then "Connecting…", the card on "TV out of reach"), even for a one-second blip; and a key pressed meanwhile was dropped by `AtvRemote.send` with nothing said and nothing felt beyond the press's own click.
- A blip shows nothing. For 3 s after a link that was up drops, the remote stays as it was, the last app included. A reconnect is a 1 s backoff, then TLS and the configure exchange: about 1.5 to 2.5 s on a home LAN, so 3 s covers the first retry with margin.
- Past 3 s, quietly: the chip's dot becomes the open ring with "Reconnecting to the TV…" (onSurfaceVariant, never the error colour), and the card says "Reconnecting…" over "Keys work again once the TV answers", with the turning-arrow glyph, not the warning triangle.
- Past 20 s (the 1, 2, 4 and 8 s backoffs and their attempts), the stronger state comes back: "BRAVIA 8 unreachable" in the chip, "TV out of reach" on the card, the out-of-reach row buttons. A retry after that never flickers back to "Connecting…". A link never up this session (the first connect), Needs pairing and no TV show as they are, with their own messages and actions.
- A link that is down wins on the card over a channel or input that REST still reports: the card vouches for the keys. Standby still wins over everything.
- A TV key pressed while the link isn't up never vanishes. One-shot keys whose effect doesn't depend on focus (Home, Back, play/pause, play, pause, stop, Guide, Settings, All apps, TV) wait up to 2 s and go the moment the link is back, newest wins: a soft bump and "Sending when the TV is back…". Everything else is dropped with the reject haptic and "Not sent: waiting for the TV": D-pad and OK (a burst of arrows landing late, or OK on whatever took focus), digits and channel steps, colour keys, skips, long presses, and power, which has its own wake path. A held key that doesn't make it says "Not sent: the TV didn't come back in time". Past give-up it is "Not sent: the TV is out of reach", and with pairing lost "Not sent: pair the TV again". The same words don't repeat while they still show, so a burst of arrows is one message and a reject per press.
- The bar's keys (volume, mute) are their own link and never wait on the TV's.
- TalkBack hears the change once, when the shown state moves: "Reconnecting to the TV…", "BRAVIA 8 unreachable", then "Connected to the TV again"; never on a blip, never at the first connect. The card's change is its usual opacity cross-fade, which is also the reduced-motion form; the ring stops turning under reduced motion, as before.

## Scenes (templates)
- A scene holds any subset of TV settings (input or app, picture mode), bar settings (sound field, voice, night, bass, volume, optional volume cap) and a final action (launch an app).
- Main creation path: "Capturer l'état actuel" — every captured value becomes a removable chip; name, colour and glyph. Blank form is the fallback.
- Per-app profiles: "Quand Netflix est au premier plan, appliquer Cinéma" (while connected).
- Strip: pill in its own tonal colour. Gallery: large card with plain-words summary ("Atmos · Voix + · Basses −2"). Active scene filled; manual change → outline marked "modifiée". Applying fills the pill left to right (progress).
- Starter gallery: Cinéma, Série du soir, Nuit (volume cap 20), Musique, Sport, Jeu, Info.

## Keyboard
- Auto-open when the TV reports a focused text field (IME show request); the pad shrinks above, still live.
- Mirror field at the top showing the TV's current text and label ("Rechercher sur YouTube").
- Each keystroke sent live; Enter carries the TV's action. Chips: Coller, Dicter. Closes when the TV field loses focus or on swipe down.
- Hardware keyboard: arrows → D-pad, Enter → OK, Esc → Back, text → IME.

## Quick access (v1)
Quick Settings tile (opens a compact remote overlay), phone volume buttons drive the bar while the app is in front (and while the notification shows, below), home-screen widget (scenes + volume), media-style notification.

### Notification
- Media style, backed by a MediaSession (`quick/notification/`; rules in `MediaCard.kt`, unit-tested). Shown while the TV is on, the room's link is up and the setting is on; Android 13+ also needs POST_NOTIFICATIONS. No foreground service: it mirrors the controller, it does not keep it alive.
- Title: the TV's foreground app ("YouTube"), else the active scene, else tutti. Text: the configured devices, then whose level it is: "BRAVIA 8 · Bar 9 · Bar vol. 10". Large icon: the app's cached icon, else tutti's mark. Lock screen: public; nothing on it is a secret.
- One volume rule for every quick surface: the bar when one is configured, else the TV. A level not yet reported is unknown and left out, never shown as 0; the TV's remote link reports `0/0` while its sound goes to the bar, so a TV level with no range is unknown too.
- Buttons: play/pause (always the TV), vol −, mute, vol +. Mute shows the state (struck speaker while muted); its label says what a tap does. Collapsed (Android 12 and older): play/pause, vol −, mute; down rather than up because turning it down is the urgent one. Android 13+ builds its controls from the session: vol − and vol + take the skip slots either side of play/pause, mute the next. The TV does not report play/pause, so the session always reads "playing" and the button is a toggle.
- Volume keys: a remote, absolute VolumeProvider (0..the bar's cap, or the TV's max) makes the phone's keys drive the bar (or the TV) whenever the notification shows (Erwann, 2026-10-24, answering the long-pending question). Android sends keys to the most recent playing session, so a phone app playing its own media takes them back while it plays; with nothing playing on the phone, the keys stay on the room, ringer included, lock screen included. If that proves too eager, the narrower scope (only while tutti is in front) is one switch: `VolumeKeysScope.IN_FRONT` in `QuickNotifications`, which then needs an app-in-front signal.
- Posts are held 400 ms apart, trailing, and an unchanged card is never re-posted: Android silently sheds updates past about five a second, and the shed one can be the last.

## Visual
- Material 3 Expressive, restrained: dynamic colour, springy shape changes, big rounded targets. Expressive in motion, not decoration.
- Dark first: near-black surface, optional true black for OLED, no white flashes. Light theme secondary.
- Pad: one large quiet surface, no grid lines, the same rounded rectangle (36 dp corners, surface-container-high) in both modes, so the D-pad and the touchpad are one size and switching never grows or shrinks the control (Erwann, 2026-10-24). D-pad mode puts a disc on that surface a tone up (surface-container-highest), as wide as the surface's short side allows, with a chevron at each point and an OK centre set off by a thin moat. Only the drawn disc answers (Erwann, 2026-10-24: "on devrait activer les flèches que quand on touche les flèches"): OK in the centre zone, otherwise the direction of the quarter between the diagonals, out to the disc's edge; the surface round it is inert in D-pad mode, and the switch's corner only switches (`dpadZone`). The one under the finger lights its quarter of the ring until release. Volume second loudest. Everything else tonal, not outlined.
- Haptics are primary feedback: light tick per D-pad/swipe step, firmer click on Select, soft bump at volume limits/cap, double pulse when a scene finishes.
- Motion: springs under ~250 ms; reduced motion → fades.
- Launch: the system splash shows the splash lockup still, the launcher mark over the lowercase "tutti"; an in-app intro (`ui/intro/LaunchIntro.kt`) takes over at the same place and size and the OK becomes the pad: it warms and slides onto the D-pad's OK while a shimmering ring runs out and morphs into the pad's surface, the disc filling on it, or, in touchpad mode, to the touchpad's centre while the ring morphs into the pad's rounded rectangle and the OK sinks into the surface. The word is on the very first frame (Erwann, 2026-10-24: "pourquoi tutti est pas affiché au début aussi"): it is baked into the splash icon (`splash_icon.xml`), the one picture both Android 12+'s splash and core-splashscreen's compat splash on Android 11 show, rather than `windowSplashScreenBrandingImage` (12+ only, at the bottom of the screen) or a word that pops in with the intro. Both splashes draw the icon's 108 units over 288 dp and show only a 192 dp circle of it (36 units round the centre), so the lockup is the mark at 0.65 with its middle on the centre and a 58-unit word 10 units under the handset, reaching 34.8 of those 36 units: on screen the handset is about 100 dp tall and the word about 100 dp wide (x-height about 27 dp), smaller than the old mark-only splash (155 dp) so the name fits. The intro places itself on 288 dp centred on the splash's icon view, whatever that view's size (288 dp on the compat splash, the 192 dp circle on 12+), so its first frame is the same lockup at the same place. On a cold start the intro then holds PR #11's splash composition (Erwann, 2026-10-24: "oui, fais-le avec le mot et le halo", then "le splashscreen est trop rapide"): the lockup never moves, and on a dark splash a soft halo in the theme's accent (peak 0.16, dithered, no banding; none in light, as in the study) breathes in behind it over 300 ms. The hold lasts until the lockup has read for about 1.2 s since the launch, the system splash's own time included, never less than 600 ms nor more than 1 s in the app; it runs while the intro waits for the stores and the remote's layout, so a slow load eats into it instead of adding to it. Word and halo leave over the choreography's first 160 ms, before the body steps back. Reduced motion: the same hold (the halo only changes opacity), then the fade takes the lockup with the mark. A warm start has no hold or halo; its word, on its splash too, leaves with the first beat. Nothing of it is announced. A click and a bloom on landing, then the backdrop opens onto the remote, already drawn underneath. The light (halo, rings, sheen, bloom) is the theme's own colour, soft and low, never white or a fixed amber. 800 ms cold, 300 ms warm, none on a resume; the first launch hands the mark to the welcome screen; reduced motion is a 200 ms fade; a tap skips it. Ivory on ink in dark, ink on ivory in light. The mark (Iris, 2026-10-24, from the v2 brand study, PR #11): a tall ivory handset (34 x 58 in the 108 viewport), a small top button, one ink OK and an amber pill, flat, inside the 66 dp safe zone; one geometry (`ui/intro/MarkGeometry.kt`) for the launcher foreground, the themed (monochrome) icon, the system splash and the intro, checked by `MarkGeometryTest`. Kept from v2: the proportion, the top button, the heavier pill. Not kept: the dial ring round the OK (two inks at about 1.6:1 vanish at 48 dp, and the intro's one moving part is a single OK), gradients and drop shadow (the launcher adds its own depth), the amber glow (now the intro's halo, in the theme's accent), and v2's palette nudges (ivory `#F4EFE7`, amber `#E5A14A`: below what the eye separates; `design/branding/README.md` stays the palette).
- Colour without Material You (Android 11 and below): Material 3's baseline scheme, which Erwann preferred to a brand scheme (2026-10-24). Android 12+ keeps dynamic colour. Either way, messages, menus, sheets and the window draw from the scheme in use, never from system defaults.
- Messages and popups (Iris, 2026-10-24): nothing tutti shows comes from the system's own look.
  - No `Toast`, no default snackbar. A passing message is tutti's message (`ui/common/Messages.kt`): a tonal card in `surfaceContainerHighest` with `onSurface` text, the theme's large shape (16 dp), bottom centre above the navigation bar, at most 560 dp wide; never the inverse colours, which flash a light bar over the dark remote. A leading glyph says its kind (info, or the warning triangle for a problem, which TalkBack also reads as "Problem"), an optional action (in developer mode, "Copy details") and a close button. One at a time; a new one replaces it. 4 s, or 8 s with an action or for a problem, stretched to the user's accessibility timeout. It rises 16 dp on a spring and fades; reduced motion is the fade alone. It is a live region (polite, assertive for a problem) with a dismiss action.
  - The host sits at the app root, and every `TuttiSheet` carries its own, since a sheet is a window above the root; only the topmost host draws. A dialog confirms in place instead (the crash dialog's Copy becomes "Copied" with a tick).
  - Menus go through `TuttiMenu`: medium shape (12 dp) on `surfaceContainerHigh`. Dialogs are Material 3's own, drawn from the theme.
  - A failed action offers "Try again" in the button's normal tonal dress with a retry glyph; the failure is told by the shake, the reject haptic and the warning line under it, never by a red button with a ✕ (which reads as cancel).
  - The XML theme follows the system's day and night (`values-night/themes.xml`), with the brand's window and accent colours, so framework-drawn pieces (text selection, a window before Compose) match.
