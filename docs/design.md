# Design direction (Iris)

The app replaces BRAVIA Connect day to day, with a Google TV–grade remote at its heart. Setup and room calibration stay in BRAVIA Connect.

## Information architecture

Three places, opening on the remote.

1. **Remote (home)**
   - Top: one system chip, "Salon · BRAVIA 8 + Bar 9", single status dot; it splits into two lines only when one device drops ("Bar 9 injoignable"). One Power button switches both.
   - Below: a horizontal strip of scene pills.
   - Under the pills, in the room the pad leaves: the now-showing card (Iris's option A, chosen by Erwann, 2026-10-24), centred in that room so the band reads as meant, never as a void. Full width less 16 dp margins: the app's icon (or the state's glyph), "On the TV", the name large and a small detail line. It says an app, a channel and programme, or an HDMI input (`nowShowing`), else "TV on standby" (with "The bar stays on" when the bar plays), the home screen, or "TV out of reach"; never empty, and every state is told by its icon and words, never colour alone. Full is 104 dp tall and grows to 140 dp (larger icon and name) when the room allows, with at least 12 dp above and below; below that room it shrinks to one 56 dp line, and below that it hides (`placePad`, `cardFor`). While it shows, the system chip drops its "On the TV" line; hidden, the chip gets it back. A tap does nothing for now. State changes cross-fade by opacity only, which is also the reduced-motion form.
   - The pad mode switch (K1, Erwann, 2026-10-24): one quiet glyph on the pad's surface, in its top corner facing the rail (top-left for the right hand, top-right for the left), that leaves the pad's shape alone. It shows the mode a tap switches to, as that mode's outline icon (the touchpad's in D-pad mode, the D-pad's in touchpad mode), 24 dp, on-surface-variant, 16 dp in from the top and side edges, with no chip, border or label; the pad already says the current mode. A 48 dp hit area round it; pressed, the glyph tints and tightens. A still tap there switches with a select haptic and never reaches the TV as OK; a touch that starts there and moves is an ordinary swipe, and a swipe crossing it tracks as normal. In D-pad mode the disc shrinks a little on a near-square pad so its reach never touches the corner, on either side (`dpadFaceRadius`, tested). The pad morphs by a spring, or a fade when motion is reduced. One button to TalkBack: the state is the current mode, the action "Switch to touchpad" / "Switch to D-pad", with an action for each mode.
   - Low, within the thumb's reach (Erwann, 2026-10-24: the thumb reaches about half the screen): the pad, the largest element, under the holding hand's thumb, with the volume rail on its other side at exactly its height, both standing 24 dp above the button row. Right hand (the default, Erwann, 2026-10-24): pad on the right, rail on the left. Left hand: pad on the left, rail on the right. TalkBack reads the pad, then the rail, on either side. A 180 dp band along the pad's outer side, centred, keeps Android's back gesture off it (`padEdgeExclusion`, under the 200 dp per-edge budget), so a swipe from the pad's edge steps. Free room goes above the pad, as the now-showing card's room, never below the pad, and the pad never grows taller than its width (Compact 0.85, Large 1.2), so a tall screen doesn't stretch it upward (`placePad`). Touchpad or D-pad. Tap = OK. Long-press Select is reserved for the TV (sends a long press).
   - Under the pad: Back, Home, Play/Pause, Mic, Keyboard, spread evenly. Mute is the volume rail's foot: the speaker there is a 48 dp key on a faint disc, the icon at full strength; a tap toggles mute, muted shows the slashed speaker over the hatched fill, and with the bar unreachable the rail is a hollow track and the speaker can't be tapped. The rail's drag starts only past touch slop, so a tap never moves the level, and a drag that starts on the speaker cancels the tap. The mic is hold to talk to the TV's assistant: it grows and fills with the primary colour while listening, a pill above it reads "À l'écoute… relâchez pour envoyer"; sliding a finger off turns it to the error container ("Relâchez pour annuler"), with a tick at the edge. A tap shorter than 300 ms sends nothing and explains the gesture. (Maya's placement, pending Iris's review.)
   - Beside the pad, on the side away from the thumb: vertical volume rail (soundbar volume, absolute), the pad's height, top and foot level with it, the mute speaker at its foot.
   - Bottom: a dock of 4 shortcut slots.
   - Peek sheet "Son" at the bottom; pulled up it shows sound controls.
2. **Scenes**: gallery and editor.
3. **Réglages**: devices and pairing, TV settings (picture, sound output, inputs, power saving), soundbar system settings, app preferences, haptics.

One tap from the remote: power, volume, mute, input, keyboard, any scene, any shortcut, Night, Voice.
Two levels deep at most.

### "Son" sheet
Sound field, Voice, Night, DRC, Auto volume, IMAX, 360 Spatial as large toggles; Bass and Rear level as sliders; Voice Zoom 3 as a level slider only if the bar answers the capability probe. A greyed setting always shows its reason ("Voix désactivée : virtualiseur actif"), never hidden. "Recalibrer dans BRAVIA Connect" deep link.

### Sheets
Every sheet (Son, All keys, Apps, a pin's editor, the action picker) can rise to just below the status bar and no further: its handle and title always show, and what doesn't fit scrolls inside the sheet. Dragging down from the top of that scroll moves the sheet. Modal sheets go through `TuttiSheet` (`ui/common/Sheets.kt`).

### Shortcuts
- Dock: four pins a page, up to three pages (swipe; dots only from page 2). Each pin has a name, an icon or two letters, a tap action (one action, or ordered steps with waits, tried from the editor with "Try") and its own long-press action, offered first in a menu above "Edit this pin".
- Long-press on any dock slot or secondary button (Home, Back, Play) assigns a second action.
- Pad gestures, two only: two-finger tap = play/pause; two-finger vertical drag = bar volume.

### Edit mode
- Settings › Shortcuts › "Remote layout", or "Edit the remote layout" at the foot of a pin's editor, turns the remote into its own edit mode: the same screen, what can change in a dashed frame, what can't under a lock. Changes save as they are made; "Done" (or Back) leaves.
- Adjustable: the round-button row (up to six, from Back, Home, Play/Pause, Voice, Keyboard, Info, Subtitles, Input, Guide, TV settings; drag sideways or TalkBack's move left / right), the scene strip (shown or hidden), the pad in three steps (Compact is shorter, Normal is as tall as it is wide, Large takes the margins back and stands taller; the rail follows the pad's height in each), the hand (Left / Right, one switch; it mirrors only the pad and the rail, never the row, Power, the scenes, the dock or the Son sheet), and the pins' order within a page (drag, or move left / right; tap opens the pin's editor).
- Anchors never move or hide: Power, the volume rail, the pad's place and the Son sheet. The pad's side is chosen once, by the hand, never dragged; the rail takes the other side. "Reset the layout" asks first and puts the row, the scenes, the pad and the hand (right) back; pins stay.
- Stored with every field defaulted to today's screen, so a phone that never opens edit mode sees no change.

## Scenes (templates)
- A scene holds any subset of TV settings (input or app, picture mode), bar settings (sound field, voice, night, bass, volume, optional volume cap) and a final action (launch an app).
- Main creation path: "Capturer l'état actuel" — every captured value becomes a removable chip; name, colour and glyph. Blank form is the fallback.
- Per-app profiles: "Quand Netflix est au premier plan, appliquer Cinéma" (while connected).
- Strip: pill in its own tonal colour. Gallery: large card with plain-words summary ("Atmos · Voix + · Basses −2"). Active scene filled; manual change → outline marked "modifiée". Applying fills the pill left to right (progress).
- Starter gallery: Cinéma, Série du soir, Nuit (volume cap 20), Musique, Sport, Jeu, Info.

## Keyboard
- Auto-open when the TV reports a focused text field (IME show request); the pad shrinks above, still live.
- Mirror field at the top showing the TV's current text and label ("Rechercher sur YouTube").
- Each keystroke sent live; Enter carries the TV's action. Chips: Coller, Dicter. Closes when the TV field loses focus or on swipe down.
- Hardware keyboard: arrows → D-pad, Enter → OK, Esc → Back, text → IME.

## Quick access (v1)
Quick Settings tile (opens a compact remote overlay), phone volume buttons drive the bar while the app is in front, home-screen widget (scenes + volume), media-style notification with volume, mute and active scene.

## Visual
- Material 3 Expressive, restrained: dynamic colour, springy shape changes, big rounded targets. Expressive in motion, not decoration.
- Dark first: near-black surface, optional true black for OLED, no white flashes. Light theme secondary.
- Pad: one large quiet surface, no grid lines. D-pad mode is a tonal disc with a chevron at each point and an OK centre set off by a thin moat; the drawn zones are the hit zones, and the one under the finger lights until release. Volume second loudest. Everything else tonal, not outlined.
- Haptics are primary feedback: light tick per D-pad/swipe step, firmer click on Select, soft bump at volume limits/cap, double pulse when a scene finishes.
- Motion: springs under ~250 ms; reduced motion → fades.
- Launch: the system splash shows the launcher mark still; an in-app intro (`ui/intro/LaunchIntro.kt`) takes over at the same place and size and the OK becomes the pad: it warms and slides onto the D-pad's OK while a shimmering ring runs out and becomes the disc, or, in touchpad mode, to the touchpad's centre while the ring morphs into the pad's rounded rectangle and the OK sinks into the surface. On a cold start the intro first opens on PR #11's splash composition, now built in the app (Erwann, 2026-10-24: "oui, fais-le avec le mot et le halo"): from the system splash's own picture, the mark never moving, the lowercase "tutti" wordmark (outlines from the v2 lockup, no font; its i dotted in the pill's amber, a separate shape) rises 4 units into place under the mark in the mark's own ink, and on a dark splash a soft halo in the theme's accent (peak 0.16, dithered, no banding; none in light, as in the study) rises behind it, 200 ms, while the intro waits for the stores and the remote's layout, so it adds at most 200 ms and usually less. Both leave over the choreography's first 160 ms, before the body steps back; the wordmark is left out when it would not clear the window's bottom (a phone held sideways). Reduced motion: opacity only, then the fade takes it with the mark. A warm start has no opening. Nothing of it is announced. A click and a bloom on landing, then the backdrop opens onto the remote, already drawn underneath. The light (halo, rings, sheen, bloom) is the theme's own colour, soft and low, never white or a fixed amber. 800 ms cold, 300 ms warm, none on a resume; the first launch hands the mark to the welcome screen; reduced motion is a 200 ms fade; a tap skips it. Ivory on ink in dark, ink on ivory in light. The mark (Iris, 2026-10-24, from the v2 brand study, PR #11): a tall ivory handset (34 x 58 in the 108 viewport), a small top button, one ink OK and an amber pill, flat, inside the 66 dp safe zone; one geometry (`ui/intro/MarkGeometry.kt`) for the launcher foreground, the themed (monochrome) icon, the system splash and the intro, checked by `MarkGeometryTest`. Kept from v2: the proportion, the top button, the heavier pill. Not kept: the dial ring round the OK (two inks at about 1.6:1 vanish at 48 dp, and the intro's one moving part is a single OK), gradients and drop shadow (the launcher adds its own depth), the amber glow and the wordmark on the system splash (it takes an icon only; both now arrive in the intro's opening instead, the glow in the theme's accent), and v2's palette nudges (ivory `#F4EFE7`, amber `#E5A14A`: below what the eye separates; `design/branding/README.md` stays the palette).
- Colour without Material You (Android 11 and below): Material 3's baseline scheme, which Erwann preferred to a brand scheme (2026-10-24). Android 12+ keeps dynamic colour. Either way, messages, menus, sheets and the window draw from the scheme in use, never from system defaults.
- Messages and popups (Iris, 2026-10-24): nothing tutti shows comes from the system's own look.
  - No `Toast`, no default snackbar. A passing message is tutti's message (`ui/common/Messages.kt`): a tonal card in `surfaceContainerHighest` with `onSurface` text, the theme's large shape (16 dp), bottom centre above the navigation bar, at most 560 dp wide; never the inverse colours, which flash a light bar over the dark remote. A leading glyph says its kind (info, or the warning triangle for a problem, which TalkBack also reads as "Problem"), an optional action (in developer mode, "Copy details") and a close button. One at a time; a new one replaces it. 4 s, or 8 s with an action or for a problem, stretched to the user's accessibility timeout. It rises 16 dp on a spring and fades; reduced motion is the fade alone. It is a live region (polite, assertive for a problem) with a dismiss action.
  - The host sits at the app root, and every `TuttiSheet` carries its own, since a sheet is a window above the root; only the topmost host draws. A dialog confirms in place instead (the crash dialog's Copy becomes "Copied" with a tick).
  - Menus go through `TuttiMenu`: medium shape (12 dp) on `surfaceContainerHigh`. Dialogs are Material 3's own, drawn from the theme.
  - A failed action offers "Try again" in the button's normal tonal dress with a retry glyph; the failure is told by the shake, the reject haptic and the warning line under it, never by a red button with a ✕ (which reads as cancel).
  - The XML theme follows the system's day and night (`values-night/themes.xml`), with the brand's window and accent colours, so framework-drawn pieces (text selection, a window before Compose) match. The window then follows the theme Compose draws, and that colour is kept for the next cold start (`WindowBackground`), so an in-app Dark or Light choice never flashes the other.
- Performance target: visual and haptic response within one frame of a tap, before any network round trip.
- UI in English by default (`values/`), French as a translation (`values-fr/`); every string in both.
